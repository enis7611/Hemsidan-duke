Multi-Stage Supply Chain

A sequential supply chain model with warehouse, transportation, and retail stages. Each stage has its own processing time and queue, representing the flow of goods from supplier through distribution to end customer.

Logistics & Supply Chain

When to use this

  • Modeling end-to-end supply chain flows from supplier to customer across multiple stages
  • Analyzing lead times and inventory levels at each stage of the supply chain
  • Identifying bottleneck stages that constrain delivery performance
  • Evaluating the bullwhip effect and demand variability amplification upstream
  • Comparing supply chain configurations (number of distribution centers, transport modes)

Common mistakes

  • ⚠️ Modeling supply chain stages with constant (zero-variability) processing times — real supply chains have significant variability in picking, transport, and receiving times; constant times underestimate queue buildup and lead time variability
  • ⚠️ Not including Queue blocks between stages — items would flow straight through without buffering, preventing the model from capturing queuing and delay effects that are the primary source of supply chain variability
  • ⚠️ Treating the model as a single flow without modeling stockouts or capacity limits — if any stage has finite capacity (e.g., truck capacity), add Queue maximum capacity limits and handle overflow items explicitly
  • ⚠️ Using the same time units for all stages without conversion — warehouse operations may be in hours while transport is in days; ensure simulation time units are consistent or use time_convert tool to align

Utilization at each processing stage

Variations

🔀

With inventory control (reorder point) →

Add Gate blocks at each stage to hold orders when inventory is below reorder point; model replenishment as a separate item stream

🔀

With shared transport resources →

Add Resource Pool for trucks or transport capacity; Transport Activity requests from pool, limiting how many shipments can be in transit simultaneously

🔀

With shift-based warehouse operations →

Add Shift block to Warehouse Activity to restrict processing to working hours; queue accumulates during off-hours
