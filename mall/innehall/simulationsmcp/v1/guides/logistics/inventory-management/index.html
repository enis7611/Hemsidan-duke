Inventory Management (Reorder Point)

A reorder-point inventory model where a Gate block checks stock level and either fulfills demand immediately from stock or places a replenishment order. Models the classic (s, S) or (R, Q) inventory policy.

Logistics & Supply Chain

When to use this

  • Modeling an inventory system with a defined reorder point and order quantity policy
  • Analyzing service level (fill rate) and stockout frequency under variable demand
  • Evaluating the trade-off between safety stock levels and holding costs
  • Modeling any threshold-triggered replenishment system (kanban, min-max, reorder point)
  • Determining optimal reorder point and order quantity for a given service level target

Common mistakes

  • ⚠️ Modeling inventory as item count without a Gate — without a Gate to block demand when stock is zero, items flow through freely regardless of stock level, making stockouts invisible in the model
  • ⚠️ Using a single Create block for both demand and replenishment instead of modeling the reorder trigger explicitly — replenishment should only occur when stock falls below the reorder point, not on a fixed schedule independent of demand
  • ⚠️ Not modeling backorders separately from lost sales — if the model routes unfulfilled demand to Exit rather than OrderBacklog Queue, it is modeling lost sales (not backorders); choose the correct stockout behavior for your scenario
  • ⚠️ Ignoring the variability in replenishment lead time — using a constant lead time when real lead times are variable underestimates required safety stock and overestimates achievable service levels

Variations

🔀

Lost sales (no backorder)

Remove OrderBacklog Queue; add a second Exit for LostSales; route excess demand directly to LostSales when Gate is closed

🔀

Periodic review (R, S) policy

Replace continuous Gate monitoring with a scheduled Create that triggers inventory review at fixed intervals; order up to S when inventory falls below s

🔀

Multi-echelon supply chain →

Chain multiple inventory stages (retail, distribution center, supplier) with each stage having its own reorder logic and lead time
