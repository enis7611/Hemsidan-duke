Inventory Management (Reorder Point)

A reorder-point inventory model where a Gate block checks stock level and either fulfills demand immediately from stock or places a replenishment order. Models the classic (s, S) or (R, Q) inventory policy.

When to use this

  • Modeling an inventory system with a defined reorder point and order quantity policy
  • Analyzing service level (fill rate) and stockout frequency under variable demand
  • Evaluating the trade-off between safety stock levels and holding costs
  • Modeling any threshold-triggered replenishment system (kanban, min-max, reorder point)
  • Determining optimal reorder point and order quantity for a given service level target

Blocks

BlockLibraryLabelPurpose
CreateItem.lbrDemandOrdersGenerates customer demand events (orders); each item represents one demand unit
QueueItem.lbrOrderBacklogHolds demand orders that cannot be immediately fulfilled due to stockout; represents backorders
GateItem.lbrStockCheckOpens when stock is available; closes when stock is depleted below reorder point, triggering replenishment
ActivityItem.lbrFulfillOrder fulfillment processing time (picking, packing); represents the time to fulfill from available stock
CreateItem.lbrReplenishmentGenerates replenishment arrivals after lead time; represents supplier delivery
ActivityItem.lbrReplenishLeadModels replenishment lead time from order placement to stock arrival
ExitItem.lbrFulfilledDemandRecords fulfilled orders; throughput versus DemandOrders rate gives fill rate

Connections

  • DemandOrders (ItemOut) → OrderBacklog (ItemIn)
  • OrderBacklog (ItemOut) → StockCheck (ItemIn)
  • StockCheck (ItemOut) → Fulfill (ItemIn)
  • Fulfill (ItemOut) → FulfilledDemand (ItemIn)
  • Replenishment (ItemOut) → ReplenishLead (ItemIn)
  • ReplenishLead (ItemOut) → StockCheck (value connector to open/close)

Key parameters

BlockParameterTypicalWhat it controls
GateinitialState / openValue / closeValueinitialState=open (stock available), controlled by inventory level signalGate opens when stock is available and closes on stockout. In practice, link a Value connector from an inventory counter to the Gate to implement the reorder-point check.
Create (Replenishment)distribution / arg1Triggered by stockout event, not time-basedReplenishment orders are triggered when stock hits the reorder point. Model as scheduled or use an Equation block to trigger replenishment based on inventory level.
Activity (ReplenishLead)distribution / arg1Constant or triangular (1-5 days)Supplier lead time from order placement to stock receipt. Longer and more variable lead times require higher safety stock to maintain the same service level.
Activity (Fulfill)distribution / arg1Constant or short exponential (0.5-2 hours)Order fulfillment processing time. This is typically much shorter than replenishment lead time and contributes minimally to stockout duration.

Common mistakes

  • Modeling inventory as item count without a Gate — without a Gate to block demand when stock is zero, items flow through freely regardless of stock level, making stockouts invisible in the model
  • Using a single Create block for both demand and replenishment instead of modeling the reorder trigger explicitly — replenishment should only occur when stock falls below the reorder point, not on a fixed schedule independent of demand
  • Not modeling backorders separately from lost sales — if the model routes unfulfilled demand to Exit rather than OrderBacklog Queue, it is modeling lost sales (not backorders); choose the correct stockout behavior for your scenario
  • Ignoring the variability in replenishment lead time — using a constant lead time when real lead times are variable underestimates required safety stock and overestimates achievable service levels

What to measure

  • Fill rate: fraction of orders fulfilled without backorder (FulfilledDemand throughput / DemandOrders throughput)
  • Average backorder level (OrderBacklog queue length over time)
  • Average stockout duration per cycle
  • Average on-hand inventory level (between replenishment cycles)
  • Replenishment frequency (number of orders placed per time period)
